What does your AI workflow really cost?

Most AI cost calculators stop at the model's bill. This one adds the people who check the AI's work, and the mistakes that get out unchecked. See the cost per 1,000 records, what a better model would have to save to pay for itself, and when a review is worth it.

01 · The bill

What do 1,000 records cost?

A record is one item through the workflow: an article, a ticket, a document. Every share below is of all records that enter, duplicates included.

The model

Tokens per call, counting everything sent (system prompt included) and everything returned, thinking tokens included: they are billed as output. Your provider's usage report or the API response's usage field has the real numbers. Calls per record is an average: 0.6 for a step 60% of records reach, 3 for a loop, 0.04 for one call per batch of 24.

The people

Use the full cost of an hour, not the wage. In the US, wages are 70% of what employees cost employers (BLS, June 2026), so divide a wage by 0.70. Minutes per review is the whole handling time: open, read, decide, fix, move on.

How it works

The math, in plain words

Model cost

Tokens in and tokens out, each at the model's price per million, for every call a record needs. Cached input is billed at the cache price, batch work at half price, and prompts over a model's long-context threshold at its higher price.

People cost

The share of records sent to a person, times the minutes each review takes, times what an hour of a reviewer's time costs. It is usually most of the bill: a review costs dollars, and a model call costs fractions of a cent.

Would a better model pay?

Every point of records sent to a person has a price. Divide the extra model cost by it, and you get how many points fewer the better model must send to a person to break even. This assumes the new model's mistakes are no more frequent.

When is a review worth it?

A review is worth it when the record's chance of being wrong, times what a mistake costs, is more than the review costs. That gives a threshold: the cost-optimal "reject" rule Chow derived in 1970. It needs a model whose confidence is calibrated.

Mistakes that get through

Your test's count of silent errors is a sample, so the page shows its likely range (95% Wilson interval, the same as the error-rate calculator), carried through to mistakes per 1,000 and what they cost.

What it leaves out

Building and maintaining the workflow, reviewer training and idle time, other per-record charges, and taxes. The method lists every assumption and when it breaks.
